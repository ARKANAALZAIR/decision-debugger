# Privacy and Decision Ledger

Capture only information necessary for the user's stated purpose.

A ledger may include:
- timestamp
- a stable, user-controlled decision identifier
- framing
- evidence state
- assumptions
- alternatives
- conditions
- reassessment triggers
- chosen option if known
- unresolved issues
- for meaningful uncertainty or observable outcomes, a locked decision-time record of forecasts and a separate, append-only outcome review
- for multiple completed records supplied by the user, evidence-linked cross-decision patterns or eligible calibration metrics

Avoid storing secrets, unnecessary identifiers, unrelated personal information, or sensitive content that does not affect the decision audit.

The ledger is manually maintained by the user. Do not imply that it persists across sessions, that reminders or monitoring run automatically, or that earlier records are available unless the runtime actually provides those capabilities. Make records copyable so the user can save and supply them again. Keep later outcome information outside the locked decision-time record. A later recollection is not a substitute for a contemporaneous forecast.
